# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Farmalengke is a global project. Contributors come from different countries,
languages, farming traditions, levels of formal education, and levels of technical
experience. We are committed to making participation a harassment-free experience
for everyone, and to treating that diversity as a strength — a maintainer in one
country does not get to decide that another region's practice is wrong simply
because it is unfamiliar.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful and assume good faith.
- Critique ideas, code, and data — not people.
- Welcome newcomers, including those who are not programmers. Field knowledge is a
  qualification here.
- Accept that people write in a second or third language. Correct the meaning, not
  the grammar, unless clarity actually depends on it.
- Give credit for others' work and sources.
- Accept feedback gracefully, and give it kindly.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Discriminatory or demeaning comments related to age, disability, ethnicity, sex
  characteristics, gender identity or expression, nationality, appearance, race,
  religion, caste, or sexual identity or orientation.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately derailing issues and reviews.
- Knowingly contributing false agronomic information or fabricated data. This
  project can affect people's livelihoods and safety, and that makes it a conduct
  issue rather than merely a technical one.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces — issues, pull requests, code
review, discussions, and documentation — and whenever someone is representing the
project in public.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, open an issue in this
repository and the maintainers will review it.

> **Maintainers:** this project does not yet have a private reporting channel.
> Public issues are a poor fit for conduct reports, which often involve details a
> reporter should not have to disclose in the open. Add a private contact address
> here before the project takes on a wider community.

Reports will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. Maintainers who
cannot handle a report impartially — for example, because it concerns them — must
recuse themselves.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards, and may
take any action they judge appropriate and proportionate, including:

1. **A private warning**, explaining what was wrong and why.
2. **A temporary ban** from interaction in project spaces.
3. **A permanent ban** for repeated or severe violations.

Maintainers may edit, remove, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other
contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and will explain their
reasoning when they do.
